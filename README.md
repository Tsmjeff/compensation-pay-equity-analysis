
# Compensation & Pay Equity Analysis

## Project Overview

This project analyzes compensation data for 1,000 employees across five departments to investigate pay patterns and potential gender-based disparities.

The analysis combines an executive Power BI dashboard with a deeper pay-equity case study covering gender, department, seniority, education, job title, performance, base pay, and bonus.

## Business Problem

The organization needed a clearer view of whether employees were being paid fairly and whether observed differences could be explained by legitimate factors such as role, education, seniority, and performance.

## Key Insights

- A **9.5% unadjusted gender pay gap** was identified across the organization.
- Male employees earned an average base salary of about **$98,458**, compared with **$89,943 for female employees**.
- The gap persisted across all five departments.
- The widest department-level gap was approximately **10.2% in Sales**.
- The gap was most pronounced at early-career levels, reaching **26.9% in Sales at Seniority Level 2**.
- Average pay increased consistently with seniority, from about **$76,840 at Level 1 to $113,478 at Level 5**.
- Performance ratings had almost no relationship with pay, with a correlation of approximately **-0.05**.
- Education and job role explained some salary variation, but did not fully explain the observed gender gap.

## Recommendations

- Conduct a formal pay equity audit with appropriate statistical controls.
- Prioritize Seniority Levels 1–2 for further review.
- Review starting salary and offer practices, particularly in Sales.
- Avoid relying solely on performance ratings to explain compensation differences.
- Use the Power BI dashboard for ongoing pay-equity monitoring.
